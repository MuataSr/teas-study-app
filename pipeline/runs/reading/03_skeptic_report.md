### Issues Found

**I1**
- **Severity:** WARNING
- **Entry reference:** [Craft & Structure] Point of View Q&A ("What is the author's point of view regarding EHRs...")
- **Description:** The answer provided ("The author believes that despite initial difficulties, EHRs are ultimately beneficial...") is actually a statement of the author's *argument or conclusion*, not their point of view. POV on the TEAS 7 is expressed as an attitude or stance (e.g., "cautiously optimistic," "nuanced support," "favorable"). This answer format will confuse students who are trying to learn the distinction between purpose, argument, and POV.
- **Suggested fix:** Change answer to: "Cautiously optimistic" or "Favorable but realistic." Revise explanation to explicitly demonstrate how POV answers use adjectives describing attitude, not summary statements of belief.

**I2**

- **Entry reference:** [Craft & Structure] Text Structure & Transitions

- **Suggested fix:** Replace with a verifiable source. Suggested: "Meyer, B. J. (1975). 'The Organization of Prose and Its Effects on Memory.' *Journal of Educational Psychology*" or remove the citation and rely solely on the ATI manual.

**I3**

**I4**
- **Severity:** WARNING
- **Entry reference:** [Key Ideas & Details] Handwashing opinion Q&A
- **Description:** Internal inconsistency with the KB's own Fact vs. Opinion misconception entry. That entry correctly states that "A fact is a statement that can be objectively verified or proven true/false through evidence" and gives an example of a "false fact." But this Q&A labels "Handwashing is the cornerstone of infection control" as an opinion because "cornerstone" is "a metaphor expressing a value judgment." The problem: whether handwashing is the foundational element of infection control *is* empirically verifiable (you could measure relative impact of various interventions). The KB creates confusion about whether metaphorical language automatically converts a verifiable claim into an opinion.
- **Suggested fix:** Either (a) add nuance to the explanation acknowledging this is a gray area on standardized tests, or (b) choose a clearer example of opinion like "Handwashing is the most important thing a nurse can do" (pure value judgment about importance relative to other duties).

**I5**
- **Severity:** INFO
- **Entry reference:** [Craft & Structure] EHR text structure Q&A
- **Description:** The explanation claims the passage "is not strictly cause-effect because the focus is on overcoming an obstacle." This misrepresents text structure categories. Problem-Solution structures inherently contain cause-effect relationships (the solution *causes* the improvement). The distinction should be that the *organizing principle* is "obstacle → response → outcome" rather than "cause → effect" in isolation.
- **Suggested fix:** Revise explanation to: "While it contains cause-effect elements (training caused improvement), the overarching structure is Problem-Solution because the passage is organized around identifying an obstacle and describing how it was addressed, not merely explaining why something happened."

**I6**
- **Severity:** INFO
- **Entry reference:** [Craft & Structure] Figurative Language misconception
- **Description:** The source Lakoff & Johnson (1980) is authoritative for cognitive linguistics but is not education research demonstrating that students actually make this error or that this pedagogical approach works. It supports the theoretical claim but not the "why students err" or "TEAS relevance" components.
- **Suggested fix:** Add a pedagogical source. Suggested: "Gibbons, P. (2002). *Scaffolding Language, Scaffolding Learning*. Heinemann" (which was in the scout brief and directly addresses ESL/science learners and figurative language).

### Missing Content

**G1: Visual Literacy (Charts, Graphs, Maps)**
- **Topic:** Key Ideas & Details / Integration of Knowledge & Ideas
- **Rationale:** TEAS 7 explicitly includes questions requiring students to interpret data from charts, graphs, tables, and maps. There are ZERO entries addressing this skill, which is a significant gap given that ATI includes 3-5 visual literacy questions per exam.
- **Suggested misconception:** "Students believe they can answer chart/graph questions by reading only the surrounding text without examining the visual itself."

**G2: Following Sequential Directions**
- **Topic:** Key Ideas & Details
- **Rationale:** TEAS 7 has specific questions testing whether students can follow multi-step procedural directions (e.g., medical procedures, recipes, lab instructions). The sterile gown Q&A touches on this, but there's no misconception entry addressing the common error of skipping steps or misreading conditional language ("if...then").
- **Suggested misconception:** "Students believe that if they understand the overall goal of a procedure, they don't need to track the specific sequence of steps."

**G3: Structural Word Analysis (Prefixes, Suffixes, Roots)**
- **Topic:** Craft & Structure
- **Rationale:** TEAS 7 tests word parts, particularly medical terminology (e.g., "hyper-" meaning excessive, "-itis" meaning inflammation). The Vocabulary in Context entry doesn't address this distinct question type, which relies on morphological knowledge rather than context clues.
- **Suggested misconception:** "Students believe that if they don't recognize a word, they must rely entirely on surrounding sentences, rather than breaking down the word's morphological parts."

**G4: Tone vs. Mood Confusion**
- **Topic:** Craft & Structure
- **Rationale:** The scout brief identified this (Q14) as a common trap, but the KB has no misconception entry. Students consistently confuse the author's emotional attitude (tone) with the feeling evoked in the reader (mood). This is tested on TEAS 7.
- **Suggested misconception:** "Students believe tone and mood are interchangeable terms that both describe how the reader feels while reading."

**G5: Author's Purpose vs. Point of View Conflation**
- **Topic:** Craft & Structure
- **Rationale:** The scout brief identified this as M6, noting that "purpose answer choices are verbs, while POV answer choices are adjectives." The KB has separate entries for each but doesn't address the common error of confusing the categories themselves, which leads to selecting a verb when an adjective is needed.
- **Suggested misconception:** "Students believe author's purpose and point of view are the same concept, leading them to select 'to persuade' when asked for POV or 'critical' when asked for purpose."

**G6: Source Evaluation by Domain Extension**
- **Topic:** Integration of Knowledge & Ideas
- **Rationale:** The scout brief identified this as M13. Students over-rely on .gov/.edu/.org as credibility markers. The KB's Source Bias entry doesn't address this specific heuristic error, which is directly tested on TEAS 7.
- **Suggested misconception:** "Students believe that all .edu and .gov sources are automatically credible, and all .com sources are automatically biased, without examining authorship or methodology."
