### Issues Found

- **ID:** I1
- **Severity:** CRITICAL
- **Entry reference:** [Biology] Ecology (Misconception M9)
- **Description:** The "Correct" explanation states: "Top predators actually require the greatest cumulative amount of energy..." This is scientifically false. Top predators have the *least* net energy available to them and require less energy to sustain their small population biomass compared to the massive energy captured by primary producers. 
- **Suggested fix:** Change the explanation to: "Top predators actually have the *least* amount of energy available to them. Because only about 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next (with 90% lost as heat), there is simply not enough energy left at the top of the pyramid to support a large population of predators."

- **ID:** I2
- **Severity:** CRITICAL
- **Entry reference:** [Anatomy & Physiology] Skin layers (Q&A Q9)
- **Description:** The explanation states: "...and the hypodermis is superficial fat." This is anatomically backward. "Hypo-" means "under" or "below." The hypodermis is the deepest layer of the skin, situated deep to the dermis, not superficial. The epidermis is the most superficial layer.
- **Suggested fix:** Change to: "...and the hypodermis is deep subcutaneous fat."

- **ID:** I3
- **Severity:** WARNING
- **Entry reference:** [Anatomy & Physiology] Tissue types (Q&A Q2)
- **Description:** The explanation states: "Connective tissue is highly vascular..." This is an overgeneralization. While some connective tissue (like blood and highly vascularized loose connective tissue) is richly supplied, several critical connective tissues are avascular or poorly vascularized, most notably cartilage and tendons. 
- **Suggested fix:** Change to: "Connective tissue varies in vascularization (though much of it is highly vascular), muscle tissue is specialized for contraction..."

- **ID:** I4
- **Severity:** WARNING
- **Entry reference:** [Anatomy & Physiology] Nervous system (Q&A Q3)
- **Description:** The answer provides the sequence: "Receptor → Afferent neuron → Interneuron → Efferent neuron → Effector" as the definitive correct order for a reflex arc. While this is correct for *polysynaptic* reflex arcs, it is incorrect for *monosynaptic* reflex arcs (like the classic patellar/knee-jerk reflex), which bypass the interneuron entirely (Receptor → Afferent → Efferent → Effector). 
- **Suggested fix:** Add a clarifying sentence to the explanation: "Note that this is the sequence for a polysynaptic reflex arc; simple monosynaptic reflexes (like the knee-jerk reflex) bypass the interneuron entirely."

- **ID:** I5

### Missing Content

- **Macromolecules Structure and Function:** A high-yield TEAS topic completely absent. Students constantly confuse which monomer pairs with which polymer (e.g., amino acids -> proteins, nucleotides -> nucleic acids) and the primary functions of lipids vs. carbohydrates.
- **Blood Typing and Compatibility:** Highly tested A&P concept. A misconception entry is needed for students who think Type AB blood can donate to Type A (it cannot; it can only receive from A, B, AB, and O, but donate only to AB).
- **Photosynthesis vs. Cellular Respiration:** Missing entirely. Students frequently swap the reactants and products of these two pathways (e.g., thinking plants "breathe in CO2 and breathe out O2" during both day and night, ignoring cellular respiration).
- **Metric System Conversions:** A staple of the TEAS 7 Science section (often overlapping with math). A misconception addressing the difference between milliliters, liters, and grams, or shifting decimals, is missing.
- **Insulin vs. Glucagon:** Missing from Endocrine. Students fundamentally confuse whether insulin lowers or raises blood glucose, and whether it's an anabolic or catabolic hormone.
- **Types of Immunity:** Missing from Immune system. Students confuse active vs. passive and natural vs. artificial immunity (e.g., assuming a vaccine is passive immunity because you don't get "actively" sick).
