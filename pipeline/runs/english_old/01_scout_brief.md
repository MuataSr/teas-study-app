# Scout Research Brief — TEAS 7 English & Language Usage

## Misconceptions

### Conventions of Standard English

**M1**
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English (Subject-verb agreement)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe that the noun immediately preceding the verb is always the subject, regardless of prepositional phrases separating them.
* **Correct explanation:** The true subject of a sentence is never located within a prepositional phrase. Students must cross out prepositional phrases to isolate the actual subject and match it to the correct verb (e.g., "The box of chocolates *is* on the table," not "are").
* **Common wrong answer:** Choosing "are" instead of "is" in a sentence like "The collection of ancient artifacts (is/are) on display."
* **Why students make this error:** This is driven by the cognitive "proximity error"—the brain naturally links the closest noun to the verb rather than holding the true subject in working memory across the intervening phrase.
* **TEAS 7 relevance:** ATI frequently tests complex subject-verb agreement using prepositional phrases (e.g., *of, in, between*) to separate the subject and verb.
* **Source:** Lunsford, A. A. (2020). *The Everyday Writer* (7th ed.). Macmillan.

**M2**
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English (Pronoun-antecedent agreement)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe that indefinite pronouns like "everyone," "each," and "anybody" take plural pronouns because they refer to multiple people.
* **Correct explanation:** Indefinite pronouns are grammatically singular and require singular pronouns (e.g., "Everyone must bring *his or her* textbook," not "their textbook" in strictly formal Standard English, though singular 'they' is evolving in informal contexts).
* **Common wrong answer:** Selecting "their" to refer back to "each of the patients."
* **Why students make this error:** Students rely on semantic logic (the word implies a plural group of people) rather than morphological and syntactic rules (the word lacks a plural morpheme and takes a singular verb).
* **TEAS 7 relevance:** TEAS 7 heavily emphasizes formal academic writing rules where traditional singular pronoun agreement for indefinite pronouns is strictly enforced.
* **Source:** ATI TEAS Study Manual (2022). Assessment Technologies Institute.

**M3**
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English (Semicolons)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe semicolons are used to introduce a list or to connect a dependent clause to an independent clause.
* **Correct explanation:** A semicolon's primary function is to join two closely related independent clauses without a coordinating conjunction. Colons, not semicolons, are used to introduce lists.
* **Common wrong answer:** Using a semicolon before a list, such as "Please bring the following items; scissors, tape, and bandages."
* **Why students make this error:** Students have a superficial visual understanding of punctuation, knowing semicolons are "heavy" like colons, but fail to differentiate their specific syntactic requirements regarding independent clauses.
* **TEAS 7 relevance:** Distinguishing between colons and semicolons is a highly tested "Conventions of Standard English" competency on the TEAS 7.
* **Source:** Purdue OWL. (2022). *Semicolons, Colons, and Parentheses*. Purdue University.

**M4**
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English (Fragments vs. Run-ons)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe sentence length determines sentence structure: long sentences are run-ons and short sentences are fragments.
* **Correct explanation:** A fragment lacks a main clause (missing a subject, verb, or complete thought), while a run-on contains two or more independent clauses improperly joined (fused or comma spliced), regardless of total word count.
* **Common wrong answer:** Identifying a grammatically correct, 25-word compound sentence as a "run-on" simply because it is long.
* **Why students make this error:** This stems from a heuristic where physical length is equated with structural complexity, bypassing the actual syntactic parsing of clauses.
* **TEAS 7 relevance:** The TEAS 7 requires students to identify fragments and run-ons in paragraph-level context, often using longer, academic sentences to trigger this length heuristic.
* **Source:** Beers, K. (2003). *When Kids Can't Read: What Teachers Can Do*. Heinemann.

**M5**
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English (Apostrophes)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe that an apostrophe is required to make any noun plural (e.g., "The nurse's checked the vital's").
* **Correct explanation:** Plural nouns are formed by adding -s or -es without an apostrophe. Apostrophes indicate possession (the nurse's stethoscope) or contractions (it's = it is), never simple plurality.
* **Common wrong answer:** Selecting "patient's" instead of "patients" when referring to multiple people in a sentence like "The clinic treats many patient's."
* **Why students make this error:** Phonological interference; the brain associates the vocalized /s/ or /z/ sound at the end of a word with the written apostrophe-s pattern learned early in possession/contraction instruction.
* **Source:** Foertsch, J. (1995). *The Unreasonable Ambiguity of the English Apostrophe*. Journal of Basic Writing.

### Knowledge of Language

**M6**
* **Topic:** Knowledge of Language (Active vs. Passive Voice)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe that passive voice is grammatically incorrect and must always be avoided in academic writing.
* **Correct explanation:** Passive voice is grammatically correct but stylistically weak because it obscures the actor (e.g., "The medication was administered" vs. "The nurse administered the medication"). It is only "wrong" when the assignment explicitly asks for active voice or conciseness.
* **Common wrong answer:** Revising a perfectly fine passive sentence to active voice when the question asks to identify a *grammatical* error, or labeling passive voice as a "fragment."
* **Why students make this error:** Teachers frequently overgeneralize the "never use passive voice" rule to prevent wordiness, causing students to misclassify it as a strictly syntactic error rather than a stylistic one.
* **TEAS 7 relevance:** TEAS 7 questions often ask students to "revise for clarity and conciseness," requiring students to recognize passive voice as a stylistic issue, not a grammatical one.
* **Source:** Strunk, W., & White, E. B. (2000). *The Elements of Style* (4th ed.). Longman.

**M7**
* **Topic:** Knowledge of Language (Tone and Register)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe that formal academic writing requires using archaic, multisyllabic vocabulary (e.g., "utilize" instead of "use," "commence" instead of "start").
* **Correct explanation:** Formal register is determined by precise, objective language and avoidance of slang/contractions, not by using overly complex or obsolete words. "Use" is actually preferred over "utilize" in strict academic writing.
* **Common wrong answer:** Choosing "The physician utilized a stethoscope" over "The physician used a stethoscope" when asked to select the most appropriate formal language.
* **Why students make this error:** Students confuse "formal" with "esoteric," relying on a false correlation between word length and academic appropriateness.
* **TEAS 7 relevance:** The "Knowledge of Language" section frequently tests audience-appropriate language in healthcare contexts, penalizing inflated diction.
* **Source:** American Psychological Association. (2020). *Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association* (7th ed.).

**M8**
* **Topic:** Knowledge of Language (Transitions and Cohesion)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe "however" and "on the other hand" can be used interchangeably with "therefore" to show cause and effect or addition.
* **Correct explanation:** Transition words have highly specific semantic functions. "However" indicates contrast/concession; "therefore" indicates cause/effect. Using "however" to link a cause to an effect destroys textual cohesion.
* **Common wrong answer:** Selecting "However" to complete the sentence: "The patient was allergic to penicillin; ___, the doctor prescribed amoxicillin."
* **Why students make this error:** Students learn transition words as isolated vocabulary rather than mapping them to specific logical relationships between clauses.
* **TEAS 7 relevance:** TEAS 7 tests paragraph cohesion by requiring students to select the transition word that logically matches the relationship between preceding and succeeding sentences.
* **Source:** Williams, J. M., & Bizup, J. (2014). *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace* (11th ed.). Pearson.

**M9**
* **Topic:** Knowledge of Language (Redundancy)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe that using multiple synonymous adjectives makes writing more descriptive and precise (e.g., "absolutely essential," "future plans").
* **Correct explanation:** Redundancy occurs when words repeat the same meaning without adding new information. "Future plans" is redundant because plans inherently exist in the future; "absolutely essential" is redundant because essential means absolutely necessary.
* **Common wrong answer:** Failing to identify "small in size" as an error, believing it adds necessary detail.
* **Why students make this error:** In conversational English, redundancy is used for emphasis. Students fail to shift their internal register checker from emphatic spoken language to concise written language.
* **TEAS 7 relevance:** Identifying and correcting wordiness and redundancy is a primary anchor of the TEAS 7 Knowledge of Language subsection.
* **Source:** ATI TEAS Study Manual (2022). Assessment Technologies Institute.

### Vocabulary Acquisition

**M10**
* **Topic:** Vocabulary Acquisition (Commonly confused words)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe "effect" is exclusively a noun and "affect" is exclusively a verb, applying the RAVEN mnemonic rigidly without exception.
* **Correct explanation:** While RAVEN (Remember Affect Verb Effect Noun) is a helpful baseline, "effect" can be a verb meaning "to bring about" (e.g., "to effect change"), and "affect" can be a noun referring to emotional expression in psychology (e.g., "flat affect").
* **Common wrong answer:** Automatically changing "effect a cure" to "affect a cure" because they assume "effect" cannot be a verb.
* **Why students make this error:** Over-reliance on a heuristic (mnemonic) prevents students from analyzing the specific syntactic slot the word occupies in a complex medical sentence.
* **TEAS 7 relevance:** TEAS 7 loves to test edge cases of commonly confused words, specifically using "effect" as a verb in healthcare contexts (e.g., "effecting a positive outcome").
* **Source:** Garner, B. A. (2016). *Garner's Modern English Usage* (4th ed.). Oxford University Press.

**M11**
* **Topic:** Vocabulary Acquisition (Context Clues)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe that if a word follows a comma, it is always a definition or synonym of the preceding word.
* **Correct explanation:** Commas can introduce appositives (synonyms/definitions), but they can also introduce contrasting phrases, examples, or coordinate adjectives. The surrounding logic of the sentence must dictate the clue type.
* **Common wrong answer:** Defining a target word as a synonym of the word following the comma, when the punctuation actually signals a contrast (e.g., misreading "The patient was lethargic, not energetic").
* **Why students make this error:** Students apply a superficial punctuation heuristic instead of engaging in deeper semantic processing of the entire clause.
* **TEAS 7 relevance:** Vocabulary questions on the TEAS 7 require distinguishing between contrast clues ( signaled by "but", "however"), example clues (e.g., "such as"), and definition clues (e.g., "or").
* **Source:** Nagy, W. (1988). *Teaching Vocabulary to Improve Reading Comprehension*. ERIC Clearinghouse on Reading and Communication Skills.

**M12**
* **Topic:** Vocabulary Acquisition (Word parts)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe that all suffixes indicating a "condition" are functionally interchangeable in medical terminology (e.g., -itis, -osis, -esis).
* **Correct explanation:** Medical suffixes are highly specific. "-itis" specifically means inflammation, "-osis" means an abnormal condition or state, and "-esis" refers to a condition or process. Using them interchangeably changes the medical meaning entirely (e.g., *thrombosis* vs. *thrombitis*).
* **Common wrong answer:** Defining "neurosis" as "inflammation of the nerves" instead of "a psychological disorder/an abnormal state."
* **Why students make this error:** Students overgeneralize the broad category of "suffix = condition" without memorizing the specific pathological distinctions required in medical fields.
* **TEAS 7 relevance:** The TEAS 7 explicitly tests basic medical terminology, requiring nursing students to differentiate between specific prefixes and suffixes.
* **Source:** Chabner, D. E. (2020). *The Language of Medicine* (12th ed.). Elsevier.

**M13**
* **Topic:** Vocabulary Acquisition (Commonly confused words)
* **Misconception statement:** Students believe "their" is the correct possessive pronoun for a singular, gender-neutral antecedent in strictly formal written English.
* **Correct explanation:** While singular "their" is accepted in conversational English and many modern style guides, strict traditional academic grammar (often tested on standardized exams) prefers "his or her" or rewriting the sentence to make the antecedent plural.
* **Common wrong answer:** Using "their" to refer back to "a nurse" in a highly formal TEAS passage asking for Standard English conventions.
* **Why students make this error:** Descriptive linguistics has normalized singular "their" in speech and informal writing, causing cognitive dissonance when students encounter prescriptive grammar tests.
* **TEAS 7 relevance:** TEAS 7 often tests the most conservative, formal interpretation of pronoun agreement rules to ensure students can write in rigid clinical documentation styles.
* **Source:** Curzan, A. (2014). *Fixing English: Prescriptivism and Language History*. Cambridge University Press.

## High-Yield Q&A Pairs

### Conventions of Standard English

**Q1**
* **Question:** Which of the following sentences demonstrates correct subject-verb agreement?
    A) The group of nursing students are studying for the TEAS.
    B) The group of nursing students is studying for the TEAS.
    C) The group of nursing students having studied for the TEAS.
    D) The group of nursing students were studies for the TEAS.
* **Correct answer:** B
* **Explanation:** The subject is "group" (singular), not "students" (which is the object of the preposition "of"). Therefore, the singular verb "is" is required. Option A contains a proximity error. Option C is a fragment. Option D uses a plural verb and incorrect verb tense.
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English

**Q2**
* **Question:** Read the following sentence: "Each of the patients has been notified of the schedule change." Which of the following corrections is necessary to align with Standard English conventions?
    A) Change "has" to "have."
    B) Change "patients" to "patient's."
    C) Change "Each" to "All."
    D) No correction is necessary.
* **Correct answer:** D
* **Explanation:** "Each" is an indefinite pronoun and is grammatically singular, requiring the singular verb "has." "Patients" is correctly plural as the object of the preposition. No correction is needed.
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English

**Q3**
* **Question:** Which of the following sentences is a run-on sentence?
    A) Because the clinic was closed, the patient went to the emergency room.
    B) The physician reviewed the chart; she ordered additional blood work.
    C) The medication was prescribed, however, the patient refused to take it.
    D) The nurse recorded the vital signs and assisted the physician.
* **Correct answer:** C
* **Explanation:** Option C is a comma splice. "However" is a conjunctive adverb, not a coordinating conjunction, so it cannot join two independent clauses with just a comma (it requires a semicolon before and a comma after). Option A is complex. Option B correctly uses a semicolon. Option D is a simple sentence with a compound predicate.
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English

**Q4**
* **Question:** Which of the following uses an apostrophe correctly?
    A) The x-ray's showed no fractures.
    B) The patient's family arrived shortly after the accident.
    C) The hospital has it's own pharmacy on the first floor.
    D) The doctor's schedules are posted on the bulletin board.
* **Correct answer:** B
* **Explanation:** Option B correctly uses the apostrophe to show singular possession (the family of the patient). Option A incorrectly makes a plural noun possessive. Option C uses "it's" (it is) instead of the possessive "its." Option D incorrectly makes a plural noun possessive (should be doctors' schedules if multiple doctors).
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English

**Q5**
* **Question:** Which of the following sentences contains a sentence fragment?
    A) The surgical team scrubbed in before the procedure began.
    B) Waiting for the lab results to return from the pathology department.
    C) Although the nurse was exhausted, she finished her shift.
    D) The physical therapist instructed the patient on how to use the crutches.
* **Correct answer:** B
* **Explanation:** Option B is a fragment because it lacks a main verb and an independent clause; it is a participial phrase. Options A and D are complete independent clauses. Option C is a complex sentence with a dependent clause and an independent clause.
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English

**Q6**
* **Question:** Which of the following sentences correctly uses a semicolon?
    A) The nurse needed to administer the medication; however, the doctor had not signed the order.
    B) The triage nurse assessed the following; burns, lacerations, and sprains.
    C) The patient was diagnosed with; hypertension and type 2 diabetes.
    D) The clinic is closed on Sundays; including holiday weekends.
* **Correct answer:** A
* **Explanation:** A semicolon is correctly used to join two independent clauses, and "however" is properly preceded by a semicolon and followed by a comma. Options B, C, and D incorrectly use a semicolon to introduce a list or separate a preposition/prepositional phrase, which is the job of a colon or comma.
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English

**Q7**
* **Question:** Select the verb tense that correctly completes the sentence: "By the time the surgeon arrived, the anesthesiologist ____ the patient."
    A) preps
    B) prepped
    C) had prepped
    D) has prepped
* **Correct answer:** C
* **Explanation:** The past perfect tense ("had prepped") is required to indicate an action that was completed *before* another action in the past ("arrived"). "Has prepped" is present perfect, which does not align with the past tense context.
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English

**Q8**
* **Question:** Which of the following sentences demonstrates parallel structure?
    A) The nursing student enjoys taking vitals, administering medications, and to chart patient progress.
    B) The nursing student enjoys taking vitals, administering medications, and charting patient progress.
    C) The nursing student enjoys to take vitals, administering medications, and charting patient progress.
    D) The nursing student enjoys taking vitals, the administration of medications, and to chart patient progress.
* **Correct answer:** B
* **Explanation:** Parallel structure requires that items in a series use the same grammatical form. Option B correctly uses three gerunds (-ing verbs): taking, administering, and charting. The other options mix infinitives (to chart) with gerunds or noun phrases.
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English

**Q9**
* **Question:** Which of the following sentences correctly uses a comma?
    A) The patient reported a history of hypertension, and asthma.
    B) After the wound was cleaned, the nurse applied a sterile dressing.
    C) The physician who performed the surgery, will see you now.
    D) The IV bag was almost empty, so we changed it.
* **Correct answer:** B
* **Explanation:** Option B correctly uses a comma after an introductory dependent clause. Option A incorrectly separates two items in a list (no comma needed before 'and' here, or missing a third item). Option C incorrectly separates a restrictive relative clause from its antecedent. Option D is a comma splice (requires a semicolon before "so" if treating as independent clauses, or no comma as "so" is a coordinating conjunction with no subject after it).
* **Topic:** Conventions of Standard English

### Knowledge of Language

**Q10**
* **Question:** A nursing student is writing a research paper on pediatric care. Which of the following phrases is most appropriate for a formal academic audience?
    A) Kids get really scared when they get shots.
    B) Pediatric patients frequently exhibit anxiety-related behaviors prior to immunizations.
    C) Little ones are totally terrified of needles.
    D) Children feel bad about getting their vaccines.
* **Correct answer:** B
* **Explanation:** Option B uses precise, objective, and formal language suitable for an academic paper. Options A, C, and D use informal, conversational phrasing ("kids," "little ones," "really scared," "totally terrified") inappropriate for academic registers.
* **Topic:** Knowledge of Language

**Q11**
* **Question:** Read the following sentence: "The new protocol was implemented by the hospital administration to reduce wait times." Which of the following revisions changes the sentence from passive voice to active voice?
    A) The new protocol was implemented to reduce wait times by the hospital administration.
    B) Implementation of the new protocol was done by the hospital administration to reduce wait times.
    C) The hospital administration implemented the new protocol to reduce wait times.
    D) To reduce wait times, the new protocol was implemented.
* **Correct answer:** C
* **Explanation:** To change passive to active, the doer of the action ("the hospital administration") must become the subject of the sentence, performing the verb ("implemented") upon the object ("the new protocol"). Option C achieves this. The others retain passive constructions.
* **Topic:** Knowledge of Language

**Q12**
* **Question:** Which of the following sentences contains redundant phrasing?
    A) The patient's vital signs were stable throughout the night.
    B) The nurse instructed the patient to take the medication twice daily.
    C) The physician made an accurate diagnosis of the patient's condition.
    D) The patient revisited the hospital again for a follow-up appointment.
* **Correct answer:** D
* **Explanation:** "Revisited" inherently means to visit again, making "again" redundant. Option C contains slightly inflated language but is not strictly redundant. Options A and B are concise.
* **Topic:** Knowledge of Language

**Q13**
* **Question:** Which transition word best fills in the blank to create a logical contrast between the two clauses? "The patient adhered strictly to the prescribed diet; _____, her blood sugar levels remained elevated."
    A) therefore
    B) furthermore
    C) nevertheless
    D) consequently
* **Correct answer:** C
* **Explanation:** "Nevertheless" indicates a contrast or unexpected result (concession). The sentence implies that *despite* adhering to the diet, the blood sugar was high. "Therefore," "furthermore," and "consequently" indicate cause/effect or addition, which do not fit the logic.
* **Topic:** Knowledge of Language

**Q14**
* **Question:** Which of the following sentences is written most concisely without changing the meaning?
    A) Due to the fact that the patient was experiencing severe pain, the nurse administered an analgesic.
    B) Because the patient was in severe pain, the nurse administered an analgesic.
    C) The reason the nurse administered an analgesic is because the patient was in severe pain.
    D) An analgesic was administered by the nurse due to the fact of the patient's severe pain.
* **Correct answer:** B
* **Explanation:** "Due to the fact that" and "the reason is because" are notoriously wordy phrases. Option B replaces the clunky phrase with the single subordinating conjunction "because," maintaining exact meaning and formal tone.
* **Topic:** Knowledge of Language

**Q15**
* **Question:** Which of the following sentences contains an ambiguous pronoun reference?
    A) The nurse told the patient that she needed to schedule a follow-up appointment.
    B) The nurse told the patient to schedule a follow-up appointment.
    C) The nurse scheduled a follow-up appointment for the patient.
    D) The patient needed to schedule a follow-up appointment with the nurse.
* **Correct answer:** A
* **Explanation:** In Option A, it is unclear whether "she" refers to the nurse or the patient. Options B, C, and D eliminate the pronoun entirely or clarify who is performing the action, resolving the ambiguity.
* **Topic:** Knowledge of Language

**Q16**
* **Question:** Read the paragraph: "(1) Hand hygiene is the most effective way to prevent infection. (2) ________, many healthcare workers fail to comply with proper handwashing protocols. (3) This noncompliance leads to increased rates of hospital-acquired infections." Which transition best fits in blank (2) to show an unexpected contrast?
    A) For example
    B) Similarly
    C) Paradoxically
    D) In addition
* **Correct answer:** C
* **Explanation:** "Paradoxically" highlights a situation that seems contradictory: if hand hygiene is the *most effective* way to prevent infection, one would expect 100% compliance. The fact that workers fail to comply is a paradox. The other options show continuation or example, not contrast.
* **Topic:** Knowledge of Language

**Q17**
* **Question:** A writer is drafting an email to a colleague about a patient's lab results. Which of the following represents the most appropriate tone?
    A) Hey! The labs are back and they are super bad.

    C) The patient's latest lab results show critically elevated potassium levels.
    D) It has come to my attention that the laboratory has finalized the testing procedure on the aforementioned sample.

* **Explanation:** Option C strikes the correct professional yet direct register expected in clinical communication between colleagues. Option A is too informal. Option B is overly stiff for an internal email. Option D is bloated and unnecessarily bureaucratic.

    C) Definition/Appositive clue
    D) Inference clue
